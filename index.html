About

I am a third-year PhD student in the Computer Science and Engineering (CSE) Division at the University of Michigan advised by Michał Dereziński. Prior to joining CSE, I completed a Master’s degree in Mathematics at the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) Bombay, and subsequently worked in the banking industry for three years on credit risk modeling. My research broadly focuses on developing randomized algorithms for large scale machine learning and optimization. In particular, I am interested in algorithms that are scalable, parallelizable, and capable of leveraging modern, massively distributed computing infrastructures. The theoretical foundations of my work lie in randomized numerical linear algebra (matrix sketching) and high-dimensional probability.

Research Motivation

The rapid growth of AI and modern computing has created a setting in which traditional statistical methods such as linear regression, as well as modern transformer-based approaches, are often not fully optimized for efficiency on contemporary computing platforms. This challenge is particularly pronounced in distributed and space-constrained computing environments, where existing methods can incur prohibitive time and memory costs. Moreover, these methods frequently fail to deliver the expected improvements in training efficiency when deployed on modern hardware accelerators. These limitations motivate the development of new algorithmic foundations that are both statistically efficient and computationally scalable. My research addresses this fundamental challenge of balancing statistical accuracy with computational complexity in large-scale machine learning via unlocking more efficient utilization of modern hardware accelerators, resulting in significantly faster training times.
